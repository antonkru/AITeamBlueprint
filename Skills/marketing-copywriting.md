# Skill: Marketing Copywriting

## Purpose
This skill covers the protocols, frameworks, and output conventions for writing effective marketing emails, with a focus on B2B SaaS and productivity tools targeting small business owners.

---

## Core Frameworks

### AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)
The most widely used email structure:
1. **Attention** — Hook the reader in the subject line and opening sentence.
2. **Interest** — Present a relatable problem or context the reader faces.
3. **Desire** — Show how the product solves that problem; use specific benefits, not vague features.
4. **Action** — One clear, compelling CTA.

### PAS (Problem → Agitation → Solution)
Effective for pain-point-led emails:
1. **Problem** — Name the specific frustration the audience experiences.
2. **Agitation** — Deepen the emotional weight of that problem (cost, stress, time lost).
3. **Solution** — Introduce the product as the relief.

---

## Tone and Voice — Small Business Owners

- **Peer-to-peer, not corporate.** Write as one business owner speaking to another, not as a brand broadcasting to consumers.
- **Respect their time.** Keep the email concise. Small business owners are time-poor. No padding, no jargon.
- **Speak in outcomes, not features.** "Save 5 hours a week" outperforms "Automated scheduling module."
- **Warm but credible.** Conversational tone, but grounded in concrete claims.
- **Avoid hype.** Phrases like "revolutionary," "game-changer," or "industry-disrupting" reduce trust. Use plain, confident language.
- **Personalisation signals matter.** Address the reader as "you." Reference their context (e.g., "running a small team," "juggling clients and admin").

---

## Subject Line Best Practices

- **Length:** 40–60 characters (displays well on mobile).
- **Avoid spam triggers:** Do not use ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation (!!!), or "FREE" in the subject.
- **Curiosity or specificity beats cleverness.** "Cut your admin time in half" > "You won't believe this tool."
- **A/B test two variants** when possible: one benefit-led, one question-led.
- **Preview text** (the line after the subject in inbox view) should extend the subject, not repeat it.

Examples of strong subject lines:
- "Less admin. More time for your actual business."
- "How [Tool Name] helps small teams do more with less"
- "You're probably losing 6+ hours a week to this"

## Email Body Structure

```
[Subject Line]
[Preview Text]

[Opening Hook — 1–2 sentences]
Connect immediately with a relatable scenario or bold statement.

[Problem / Context — 2–3 sentences]
Name the pain point. Make the reader feel seen.

[Solution Introduction — 2–3 sentences]
Introduce the product naturally as the answer. Lead with the primary benefit.

[Supporting Evidence — bullet list or short paragraph]
3–5 specific, concrete benefits or proof points (stats, time savings, outcomes).

[Social Proof — optional, 1–2 sentences]
A brief testimonial quote or usage stat ("Trusted by 2,000+ small businesses").

[CTA — single, prominent]
One action only. Active verb. Specific. E.g., "Start your free 14-day trial" not "Click here."

[Sign-off]
Human name + role. Adds warmth and accountability.

[PS — optional but high-read-rate]
Reinforce the CTA or add urgency: "PS — Our early-access pricing ends Friday."
```

---

## CTA Conventions

- **One CTA per email.** Multiple CTAs dilute focus.
- **Use a button where possible** (HTML emails); plain-text fallback should hyperlink a phrase.
- **Active, specific verbs:** "Start free trial," "Book a 15-min demo," "Get early access."
- **Avoid vague CTAs:** "Learn more," "Click here," "Find out."
- **Create low-friction framing:** "No credit card required," "Takes 2 minutes to set up."

---

## Output Format

When producing a finished marketing email, output must include:

1. **Subject Line** (clearly labelled)
2. **Preview Text** (clearly labelled)
3. **Email Body** (full copy, ready to send)
4. **CTA Text** (clearly labelled, even if embedded in body)
5. **Optional:** A brief note (2–3 sentences) explaining the framework choice and tone decisions made.

All copy should be ready for use with no further editing required unless the client requests changes.

---

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Subject line is under 60 characters and avoids spam triggers
- [ ] Opening hook engages within the first two lines
- [ ] Benefits are specific and outcome-focused, not feature-focused
- [ ] There is exactly one CTA
- [ ] Tone is warm, peer-level, and jargon-free
- [ ] Email can be read in under 60 seconds
- [ ] Sign-off includes a human name
