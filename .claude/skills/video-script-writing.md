# Skill: Short-Form Marketing Video Script Writing

## Purpose
Write 30–60 second marketing video scripts that stop the scroll, communicate a clear value proposition, and drive a single measurable action — formatted for immediate handoff to video producers, editors, or AI video tools.

## Core Principles

1. **Three seconds or you've lost them** — the hook is the most important line in the script; it determines whether anyone sees the rest.
2. **One message, one video** — pick a single core idea; cramming multiple messages into 60 seconds kills conversion.
3. **Write for mute** — assume up to 85% of social media viewers watch without sound; every critical word must also appear on screen.
4. **Benefit-led, not feature-led** — lead with the outcome the viewer will experience, not product specifications.
5. **Economy of language** — every word earns its place; ruthlessly cut filler.

---

## Pre-Writing Protocol

Before writing a single word of script, answer these questions:

| # | Question | Why It Matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Who is the exact target viewer? | Tone, vocabulary, and pain points depend on this |
| 2 | What is the single most important message? | Prevents scope creep in a 60-second window |
| 3 | What problem does the viewer have right now? | Grounds the hook in real emotion |
| 4 | What is the one desired action after watching? | Shapes the CTA and the entire script arc |
| 5 | Where will this video live? (TikTok / Reels / YouTube Shorts / LinkedIn / paid ad) | Determines pacing, tone, and text convention |
| 6 | Is there a voiceover, talking head, or text-only delivery? | Changes how the script is formatted |

## Standard Structure: Hook → Problem → Solution → CTA

### 1. Hook (Seconds 0–3)
The hook must interrupt the viewer's scroll in the first 3 seconds. Use exactly **one** of these techniques:

- **Question** — Ask something the target viewer is already thinking:
  *"Still paying too much for project management software?"*
- **Bold / contrarian statement** — Challenge a common assumption:
  *"Your onboarding email is why customers churn."*
- **Surprising statistic** — Lead with a specific, credible number:
  *"73% of SaaS trials never send a single task to a team member."*
- **Pattern interrupt** — An unexpected visual, sound, or opening line that breaks autopilot scrolling.

> **Rule:** Never open with the brand name, a logo, or "Hi, we're [Company]." That is the fastest way to lose a viewer.

---

### 2. Problem (Seconds 3–15)
Agitate the pain. Make the viewer feel understood. Keep it tight — 2–3 sentences maximum.

- Name the specific frustration, not a generic category of problem.
- Use "you" language, not "our customers."
- End this section with the viewer leaning in, not leaning back.

*Example:* "You've tried three different tools. Nothing syncs. Your team's working off spreadsheets from last Tuesday, and your client is asking questions you can't answer."

---

### 3. Solution (Seconds 15–45)
Present the product or service as the natural, obvious answer to the problem just established.

- **Lead with the outcome**, not the mechanism: *"With [Product], your team sees every task, deadline, and dependency in one place — from day one."*
- Include one **proof point** if time allows: a number, a customer result, a visible demo moment.
- If runtime is 30 seconds, compress Problem + Solution into 10–20 seconds total.

---

### 4. Call to Action (Seconds 45–60 / final 5 seconds for 30s videos)
One action only. Make it specific, urgent, and low-friction.

| Weak CTA | Strong CTA |
|----------|-----------|
| "Learn more" | "Start your free 14-day trial — no card needed" |
| "Visit our website" | "Click the link and see your dashboard in 2 minutes" |
| "Follow us" | "Follow for one project management tip every day" |

> **Rule:** The CTA must state the benefit to the viewer, not just the action required of them.

## Pacing Guidelines

| Video Length | Target Word Count (VO) | Comfortable Speaking Rate |
|---|---|---|
| 15 seconds | 30–38 words | ~2.0–2.5 words/sec |
| 30 seconds | 65–80 words | ~2.2–2.7 words/sec |
| 60 seconds | 120–150 words | ~2.0–2.5 words/sec |

**General rules:**
- Write to the **low end** of the word count range — this leaves breathing room for emphasis and natural delivery.
- Social/TikTok/Reels: faster delivery is expected (up to 170 WPM); LinkedIn and B2B ads: favour 130–145 WPM for clarity.
- Read the draft aloud before finalising. If you run out of breath, it's too long.
- Never write a script that runs to the exact time limit — leave 5–10% buffer for emphasis.

## Voiceover (VO) vs. On-Screen Text Conventions

### When to Use Voiceover
- Long-form narrative where pacing and emotion matter
- Talking-head videos where the speaker is on camera
- Ads on platforms where users frequently watch with sound on (YouTube pre-roll, Connected TV)

### When to Use On-Screen Text Only
- Silent-first platforms (TikTok feed, Instagram Reels, LinkedIn auto-play)
- Fast-paced montage or product demo videos
- When the visual itself carries the story and words are captions

### When to Combine Both (Recommended Default)
Most effective short-form ads use **voiceover + on-screen text reinforcement**:
- Voiceover carries narrative flow and emotion
- On-screen text **highlights** the most critical 4–8 words from each line (not a full transcript)
- Captions / subtitles provide accessibility and silent-viewing support

### Formatting Rules for Each
| Element | Convention |
|---|---|
| Voiceover line | Written in full sentences in the **Audio** column |
| On-screen text / lower-third | Written in **ALL CAPS** or [BRACKETS] in the **Visual** column, max 6–8 words |
| Music / sound cues | Italicised in the Audio column: *[Upbeat background music fades in]* |
| Visual direction | Plain descriptive text in the Visual column: *Close-up of dashboard loading instantly* |

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Opening with the brand name or logo** — viewers skip immediately
- **More than one CTA** — "follow us AND visit the site AND share this" = zero actions taken
- **Feature lists instead of benefits** — viewers don't care about specs; they care about outcomes
- **Corporate jargon** — "leverage synergistic solutions" loses every viewer in under 5 seconds
- **Writing for sound only** — always ensure critical messages survive a mute viewing
- **Exceeding word count budget** — a rushed delivery undermines trust and clarity
- **Vague CTAs** — "click below" tells the viewer nothing about why they should

---

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Hook lands within the first 3 seconds and uses a proven technique (question / bold claim / stat)
- [ ] Script addresses a specific, named problem the target viewer actually has
- [ ] Solution is benefit-led, not feature-led
- [ ] Word count is within the pacing budget for the target runtime
- [ ] Script has been read aloud — it fits in time with room to spare
- [ ] On-screen text is ≤ 8 words per card and reinforces (not duplicates) the voiceover
- [ ] Exactly one CTA, with a clear benefit stated
- [ ] Critical message survives with the sound off
- [ ] Output uses the two-column A/V format with all header fields populated

## Platform-Specific Notes

| Platform | Max Runtime | Tone | Key Consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 60s (sweet spot: 21–34s) | Casual, fast, energetic | Hook must be ultra-fast; raw/authentic style outperforms polished |
| Instagram Reels | 60–90s | Visual-first, lifestyle | Strong opening visual matters as much as opening line |
| YouTube Shorts | 60s | Educational or entertaining | Slightly longer narrative arcs acceptable |
| LinkedIn | 30–60s | Professional, credible | Slower pace; proof points and data perform well |
| Paid Ads (Meta/Google) | 15–30s | Direct, benefit-focused | CTA must be explicit; assume no brand awareness |
